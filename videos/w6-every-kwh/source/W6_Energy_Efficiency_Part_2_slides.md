### Notes:
[Bloom: C2, 5 min]
Course note 4.1.1 gives efficiency, conservation, renewable energy and recovery. We add ENERGY MANAGEMENT because engineers use it every day in factories.
Simple memory trick:
- Efficiency = same output, less energy (better equipment).
- Conservation = do not use energy you don't need (behaviour: switch off).
- Management = plan, measure, control (audits, meters, targets, SOP).
- Recovery = catch the wasted energy and use it again (waste heat).
- Renewable = where the energy comes from (solar).
Course note point: a factory can improve efficiency without installing any solar panel. They are different things.

### Notes:
[Bloom: C3, 6 min]
These four formulas are used in almost every energy calculation.
1. Energy = power x time. Units: kW x h = kWh. Example from note 4.5.1: 8 kW for 5 h = 40 kWh.
2. Efficiency = useful output / input x 100. Example: motor 800 kJ in, 600 kJ out → 75%. Energy LOST = 100 − 75 = 25%.
3. Energy intensity (note 4.5.2): 600 kWh for 300 good parts = 2 kWh per part. Always divide by GOOD parts, not total parts.
4. Percentage saving (note 4.5.3): (600 − 510)/600 x 100 = 15%.
Unit warning: kW is a rate (power). kWh is an amount (energy). Don't mix them in one formula.

CH 4  ›  4.2.3 Variable speed drives (VSDs)
VSD: slow the motor down, save a lot
![VSD controlling a motor that drives a fan](Image 0)
Why the saving is big (fans and pumps)
Power ∝ (speed)³
1
Run at 80% speed
2
A Variable Speed Drive (VSD) changes motor speed to match demand, instead of running at full speed all the time.
Power = 0.8³ = 0.51 → 51%
3
Best for: pumps and fans with changing flow; some compressors; conveyors
Limits: cost, motor compatibility, electrical harmonics; little saving if the motor already runs near full load all day
Saving ≈ 49% (ideal)
4
Real savings are lower than the ideal value. Always measure.
12

### Notes:
[Bloom: C2–C3, 5 min]
Course note 4.2.3: a VSD controls motor speed to match demand. Example from the note: a ventilation fan does not need full speed all day.
Why the saving can be large: for fans and centrifugal pumps, power is roughly proportional to speed cubed (the affinity laws). So:
- 80% speed → 0.8 x 0.8 x 0.8 = 0.512 → about 51% of the power → about 49% saving (ideal).
- Real savings are lower because of drive losses and system effects. Always measure.
Limitations from the note: cost, compatibility, harmonics, and very small savings if the motor already runs near full load continuously. This leads to the next question.

CH 4  ›  4.2.4 and 4.5.1 Worked example: CNC energy (part 1)
CNC worked example, part 1: find the power
![CNC nameplate showing 415 V 3 phase, 18 A, 7.5 kW spindle](Image 0)
Read the nameplate: 415 V, 3-phase
1

Cutting: P = √3 × 415 × 12 × 0.85 = 7,330 W = 7.33 kW
![Clamp meter reading 12.0 A on a supply cable](Image 1)

### Notes:
[Bloom: C3, 6 min. Go slowly; students will copy this method for their video]
Step 1: the nameplate (usually at the back of the machine or on the electrical cabinet door) tells us the supply: 415 V, 3-phase, 50 Hz. In Malaysia, three-phase supply is 415 V.
Step 2: the technician clamps a meter on the supply cable. Students must NOT open the electrical cabinet. Readings: 12 A when cutting, 2 A when idle.
Step 3: power factor. If unknown, assume 0.85 and say so.
Step 4: √3 = 1.732. 1.732 x 415 = 718.8. x 12 = 8,626. x 0.85 = 7,332 W ≈ 7.33 kW.
Step 5: 718.8 x 2 = 1,438. x 0.85 = 1,222 W ≈ 1.22 kW.
Note: the nameplate full-load current (18 A) is the MAXIMUM. Using it would overestimate the energy. That is why we measure, or use a load factor.

### Notes:
[Bloom: C2, 4 min]
Compressed air (note 4.2.7): producing it uses a lot of electricity. Fix leaks, reduce pressure, don't use compressed air for cleaning floors if a broom works, switch off isolated lines.

### Notes:
[Bloom: C3, 4 min]
Three ways to get the power, from easy to accurate:
1. Nameplate: shows supply voltage (415 V 3-phase in Malaysian labs, or 240 V single-phase) and full-load current or kW. These are MAXIMUM values, so multiply by a load factor and state your assumption.
2. Controller screen: many CNCs show spindle load %. Use it as your load factor.
3. Clamp meter: the most accurate, but only the technician may do it.
